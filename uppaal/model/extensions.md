# U5: extensions of the abstraction

This file states the semantics of the three extensions of work item U5
(`docs/uppaal-plan.md`, section 9). It was written before any U5 query was run.
The extensions model behaviors that T2 excludes. In T2 a command executes in the
step that issues it, and its outcome is one event (`accepted` or `lost`). One loop
issues all commands. So a trace of an extension is not a T2 run, and
U3 does not replay it. A trace that breaks a property is a candidate finding. It
waits for an extended T2 in Lean, and it can be spurious, because the contract
over-approximates oref0.

## Common structure

Each extension has two small models, generated by `build_ext` in `uppaal/model/model.py`.

- The pump part checks P4b. It has `Step`, `Pump`, the pre-run initial temp of
  U1a and the close of the run. A cycle issues one valid temp command that the
  contract allows: a cancel, a zero temp of 30 to 120 whole minutes, a low temp of
  30 minutes, or a high temp of 30 minutes. A command has no units. The delayed
  pump part uses fewer commands: a cancel, a zero temp of 30 or 120 minutes, a low
  temp and a high temp of 30 minutes. With all 91 zero durations, two commands in
  the queue made the shifted P4b queries run out of 16 GB.
- The bolus part checks P5. It has `Step`, `Controller`, `CGM`, `User`, the
  pre-run bolus and the P5-issued and P5-pair observers of U1b. A cycle issues no
  units, units ≤ 0 (`uk = 2`), or a microbolus (`uk = 3`). A command has no temp
  part. A cycle that issues no units sends no command.

The pump part has no freshness guard on the high temp. This drops no pump
behavior and adds none, because the guard can always be met. In every step,
either the last reading is younger than 150 s and so fresh, or the CGM can take
a new reading in phase 1. Readings change nothing in the pump part.

The variant `u5-base` of each part is the same model without an extension. It
is the reference: P4b and P5 must hold on it, as they hold on the U1b models.
Then a failure on an extension comes from the extension, not from the reduced
command set.

### Relation to the U1b contract model

Each part is a sub-model of the corresponding extension of the U1b contract
model: it has fewer commands (no temp part in the bolus part, no units in the
pump part, no NaN or rejected fields, and fewer durations in the delayed pump
part). So:

- a counterexample or a reachable witness transfers to the extended contract
  model. It is a behavior of that model,
- a result that holds is checked on the part only. It is argued for the full
  command set below, but not checked.
- The delayed pump part leaves out the zero durations 31 to 119 minutes. So a
  result that holds on it covers only its five commands.

The argument for the full command set maps each command of the extended contract
model to a command of the part. `validate` accepts or rejects a whole command, so
the map uses the outcome of the whole command, not each field alone:

| full command | bolus part | pump part |
|---|---|---|
| accepted, with a microbolus | a microbolus (`uk = 3`) | its temp part as below |
| finite units, rejected (any reason, units ≤ 0 included) | units ≤ 0 (`uk = 2`), also rejected | no pump effect |
| accepted, with a temp or a cancel | its units as above, or no units | the same temp class and duration, or a cancel |
| accepted, without a temp part | its units as above | no pump effect |
| NaN or absent units | no units | its temp part as above |

The P5-issued observer and the spacing guard treat `uk = 2` and `uk = 3` alike,
so the map keeps P5-issued. The map keeps every executed microbolus and every
executed temp, with its time. A command with no effect in a part maps to no
command, or to a step without a cycle. Its other effects are covered as follows:

- `delayed`: it can take a queue slot. A later command then waits behind it, but
  still executes within 300 s of its own issue. The part can execute that command
  at the same time. If the full queue drops a later command, the part loses it in
  the channel instead.
- `lostack`: it can replace a pending command, or be retried. In the part, the
  loop stops retrying the replaced command. That is always allowed, because a
  retry is optional. A retry of a command with no effect has no effect. A new
  identity only clears what the pump remembers for deduplication, which matches a
  part that stops retrying.
- `twoloops`: it has no effect beyond its step.

Each extension contains the behaviors of the `u5-base` model on the commands that
both have. The delayed pump part has fewer commands than `u5-base-pump`, so for
example a zero temp of 60 minutes is reachable only in `u5-base-pump`.
The shared behaviors are the runs with a delay of 0, with an acknowledgement for
every executed command, or with one loop only. So every U1b behavior of the
reduced command set stays in the extension.
Results of U1b that are `A[]` queries do not transfer to the extensions, because
the extensions add behaviors. Reachable U1b witnesses do transfer. The U1b and
U1a results themselves are about their own models and stay valid.

## Delayed execution (`delayed`)

- A cycle decides at its step. The decision reads the state at that step: the
  freshness of the reading, `sinceBolus` and `noBolus`. This is the decision
  snapshot. The pump state at execution can differ from it.
- If the channel accepts the command, the command enters a FIFO queue. If the
  channel loses it, it never enters the queue.
- The queue holds at most 2 commands. A command that arrives at a full queue is
  dropped. A dropped command is a lost command, which T2 already allows.
- The head of the queue executes at any time from its issue on, at most
  `DMAX` = 300 s after its issue. Execution is its own event, outside any step.
  It can happen after the close of the run. The pump applies `validate` and
  `applyCmd` at execution, to its state at that time.
- The pump history records a microbolus at its execution. So `sinceBolus` and
  `noBolus` change at execution, not at the decision. A user bolus acts at once,
  as in U1b.

Bound of the queue: 2 is the smallest bound at which the queue keeps two
commands before either executes. With 1, a command that is decided while an
earlier one waits is dropped. Two kept commands are the new behavior. A larger bound gives longer
chains of the same pattern and a larger state space.

Bound of the delay: without a bound, no time after the close is safe, so P4b
fails for every bound. `DMAX` = 300 s is one loop period of oref0. With it,
P4b has a shifted form: the bounds 7200 s and 1800 s become 7200 + 300 s and
1800 + 300 s.

Added behaviors: a temp or a microbolus executes up to 300 s after its step,
after the close, or after a later decision. Two commands can be decided before
either executes.

## Lost acknowledgement (`lostack`, `lostack-dedup`)

- A sent command has three outcomes: lost before execution, executed and
  acknowledged, or executed with a lost acknowledgement. The controller cannot
  tell the first and the last apart: in both it gets no acknowledgement.
- A command without an acknowledgement stays pending. At a later step the loop
  can retry it, decide anew (the new decision replaces the pending command), or
  do nothing.
- A retry sends the same command again, with the same identity. It is not a new
  decision: the controller does not evaluate its guards again, and the issue
  observers (P5-issued) do not see it. A retry goes through the channel again
  and has the same three outcomes.
- Retries happen only in steps of the loop. So no command executes after the
  close of the run.
- The pump history records a microbolus at its execution, acknowledged or not.
  So a new decision sees every executed microbolus.
- `lostack`: the pump executes every copy that it receives. `lostack-dedup`: the
  pump remembers the identity of the last command that it executed, and ignores
  a copy with the same identity. It still acknowledges it.

Added behaviors: a command executes at a later step than its decision, and the
same command can execute more than once.

## Two loops (`twoloops`)

- Two loops issue commands to one pump. A step belongs to one loop. Two steps
  never happen at the same instant, as in the `Step` template of U1b. This
  leaves out simultaneous steps of the two loops, so it removes behaviors.
- Each loop takes its own snapshot. A snapshot holds the freshness of the reading
  and the spacing guard (`noBolus` or `sinceBolus ≥ 183`) at the time it is taken.
  The model can also record the guard as false when it is true. This only removes
  decisions, because a decision without units is always allowed.
- A step of a loop can take a snapshot and end, decide from the snapshot that it
  holds, or take a snapshot and decide in the same step (the U1b behavior). The
  guards of the decision read the snapshot, not the current clocks. So both loops
  can decide on the same old history.
- A command executes in the step that issues it, as in T2. The close ends both
  loops.

Added behaviors: a decision whose snapshot is older than a microbolus that the
other loop issued and the pump executed.

## Expected results

These are the expectations written before the first run. Every result of the
run matches them (`queries/results.json`).

| extension | P4b-120, P4b-30 | P5-issued | P5-pair |
|---|---|---|---|
| `u5-base` | hold | holds | holds |

| `lostack` | hold | holds | fails |
| `lostack-dedup` | (not checked, the pump part has no units) | holds | holds |
